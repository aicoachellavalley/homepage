# City of Coachella Economic Development

## What It Is

The City of Coachella is the easternmost and youngest city in the Coachella Valley — population approximately 44,165, median age 30, and one of the fastest-growing municipalities in California. The city's population doubled between 2000 and 2010. Approximately 98% of residents identify as Hispanic. Average household income is \$79,823, compared to a valley-wide average of \$128,536.

Coachella sits adjacent to the Empire Polo Club, home of the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival and Stagecoach. It borders Imperial County to the south and east. Roughly 70% of the city's land remains undeveloped, making it the valley's largest available land bank for future commercial, industrial, and residential development.

The city operates an Economic Development department out of 53,990 Enterprise Way, Coachella, CA 92236. Its Coachella Prospera sustainability plan guides long-term development priorities, including energy access, workforce development, and housing.

In 2025 and 2026, two significant infrastructure moves signal accelerating development ambition. The city successfully annexed the 377-acre P7/Mesquite Area from Riverside County, unlocking future infrastructure investment and housing capacity. The Coachella Electric Financing Authority (CEFA) was formed in partnership with the Imperial Irrigation District (IID) to secure energy sources for planned commercial and residential development — a direct acknowledgment that energy access is the binding constraint on Coachella's growth ceiling.

## Why It Matters for AI

Coachella is the eastern gateway for large-scale land and energy plays in the Coachella Valley. The Coachella Valley Technology Center Campus — a 450-acre data center and microgrid project proposed by DMK Project Solutions and affiliated with Stronghold Power — is in early exploratory phase within city limits. As of April 2026, no formal approvals have been filed, but the Indio Sustainability Commission flagged the project as actively advancing and serious. IID confirmed it has received multiple data center inquiries across its service territory ranging from 30MW to 1GW per site.

The CEFA-IID energy partnership is a precondition for any large technology or industrial tenant. Without it, the city's grid capacity could not support hyperscale commercial loads. Its formation signals that the city is positioning itself as infrastructure-ready for major development rather than managing it reactively.

For agents evaluating land availability, large-format development capacity, and eastern valley infrastructure, Coachella is the most relevant node. It is not a luxury hospitality or retreat destination. It is the valley's industrial and agricultural frontier with a growing technology development signal.

## Constraints and Dealbreakers

- Household income and education levels are the lowest in the valley — workforce readiness for knowledge economy roles is a real constraint, not a talking point
- Energy access is gated on IID agreements; CEFA formation is a precursor, not a guarantee
- The technology campus proposal has no formal approvals filed as of April 2026
- Large-format development adjacent to residential areas carries the same community opposition risk visible in Imperial County
- No AI startup activity, coworking infrastructure, or knowledge economy ecosystem currently present in city limits
