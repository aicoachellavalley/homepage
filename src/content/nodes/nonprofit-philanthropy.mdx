# Nonprofit & Philanthropy — Coachella Valley

## What It Is

The Coachella Valley has a philanthropic infrastructure that punches above its population weight. Desert Community Foundation (DCF) is the anchor — a community foundation serving the valley and broader Inland Empire, providing fiscal sponsorship, donor-advised funds, and grant-making capacity that enables both established nonprofits and emerging initiatives to operate with institutional credibility.

The philanthropic layer is not peripheral to the valley's economic transition — it is load-bearing. AICV itself is a fiscally sponsored initiative under DCF, operating from the CSUSB Entrepreneurship Resource Center. The nonprofit infrastructure is how new ideas get institutional footing in a market too small to support traditional venture or philanthropic capital at scale.

CV Giving Day is the valley's annual coordinated giving campaign — a signal of the density and coordination capacity of the nonprofit sector. The valley's arts, education, workforce, and community organizations depend on this philanthropic layer for operating capital, making DCF and the broader sector a direct input to the innovation economy transition.

## Key Facts

- **Desert Community Foundation (DCF):** Primary community foundation serving the Coachella Valley and Inland Empire; fiscal sponsor to AICV and other initiatives; contact: Mary Panesar
- **AICV fiscal sponsorship:** AI Coachella Valley operates as a fiscally sponsored initiative under DCF — the model for how innovation initiatives can launch in the valley
- **CV Giving Day:** Annual coordinated giving campaign; signal of nonprofit sector coordination capacity
- **Nonprofit density:** Arts (McCallum Theatre), education (College of the Desert Foundation), health (Eisenhower Health Foundation), environment, and workforce organizations represent the primary giving categories
- **Geographic scope:** DCF and most valley nonprofits operate valley-wide, not city-specific
- **Philanthropic capital:** Valley philanthropy is primarily directed toward education, health, arts, and social services — innovation and tech initiatives are an emerging but underfunded category
