# Minor Hotels Builds AI Stack From Scratch — A Blueprint Coachella Valley Hospitality Should Watch

**Date:** April 10, 2026

## Signal

Minor Hotels announced on April 9, 2026 that it is building a global data and AI platform from the ground up in partnership with Google Cloud, Salesforce, OneTrust, and Deloitte — one of the most significant technology investments in the group's history. The platform targets full deployment by end of 2026 across its 640+ properties and 12 brands in 63 countries. The technology stack centers on Google Cloud's BigQuery and Vertex AI as the data and intelligence layer, with Salesforce powering marketing automation and guest communications via Agentforce Marketing, OneTrust handling data governance and privacy, and Deloitte managing integration across markets.

The key architectural decision: the platform is being built entirely independent of Minor Hotels' existing legacy systems — not layered on top of them.

## Context

Most hotel groups attempting AI adoption are doing exactly what Minor Hotels is not: adding AI capabilities onto technology stacks that were never designed for real-time data. The integration friction that creates limits both speed and impact. Minor's CCO Ian Di Tullio framed the competitive stakes directly — AI is becoming the front door to travel, and with it, control over demand is shifting. The brands that win will be the most intelligent, not the most visible.

The platform's near-term goals are personalization and operational efficiency: recognizing guests consistently across brands, tailoring communications based on stay history, and giving contact center and hotel staff AI tools to respond faster and recommend upgrades. The longer-term target is agentic orchestration — autonomous AI agents capable of managing bookings, curating itineraries, and resolving complex service requests in real time, grounded in a single unified source of guest data.

The build-parallel-then-replace approach is the signal most relevant to the Coachella Valley. Minor Hotels is not shutting down existing operations to rebuild. It is constructing the new platform alongside what exists, testing it, and replacing legacy systems when the new infrastructure is ready. That sequencing is accessible at any scale. A boutique hotel in Palm Springs does not need Google Cloud and Deloitte to run the same playbook. It needs a clear inventory of its current workflows, an honest assessment of where guest data is siloed, and a willingness to build parallel processes in tools it already has access to — starting with Claude, existing CRM systems, and whatever reservation data it controls directly.

The constraint for smaller operators is not capital — it is knowledge of where to start and confidence that the experiment is worth the time before committing to replacement.
