# Altman Warns AGI Could Collapse the Economy — OpenAI's Own Policy Paper Says Prepare Now

**Date:** April 27, 2026

## Signal

On April 25, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman posted on X that "post-AGI, no one is going to work and the economy is going to collapse." The statement came two days after OpenAI released GPT-5.5 on April 23 — available to paid subscribers — described by President Greg Brockman as capable of multi-step planning, tool use, and self-verification with reduced human oversight. The warning connects directly to a 13-page OpenAI policy paper released April 6, which proposed a public wealth fund, a four-day workweek pilot, a shift in taxation from labor to capital, and a robot tax. Security context: on April 10, the FBI characterized an attack on Altman's San Francisco home and a subsequent appearance at OpenAI's offices as "planned, targeted, and extremely serious."

## Context

Altman's public warning, paired with OpenAI's own structural policy proposals, signals that even the builders of these systems are modeling disruption at a scale requiring civic and workforce responses. The April 6 policy paper is not a roadmap for slowing AI — it is a contingency plan for what comes after adoption is already underway.

According to AICV, the pairing of GPT-5.5's release with Altman's economic collapse warning and OpenAI's April 6 policy paper creates a compressible signal for regional workforce planning: the company deploying the technology is simultaneously proposing labor-to-capital tax shifts and universal wealth mechanisms. The April 6 policy paper is a benchmark document — not for its proposals, but for what its existence confirms about internal OpenAI modeling.
